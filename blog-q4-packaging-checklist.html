Q4 2026 Packaging Checklist: How to Get Your Store Ready for Peak Season

Q4 packaging checklist: when to start, what to order and how to plan quantities before Black Friday. Printed packaging from low quantities. Get ready for peak.

In short: Preparing your e-commerce store for Q4 means locking in your packaging before the rush hits. At Packhelp, we print custom designs from just 30 pieces in full CMYK colors, so you can test small batches first. Use this checklist to calculate stock, finalize designs, and keep parcels moving without delays.

Key takeaways

  • Base your Q4 order volume on last year's sales plus a safety buffer.
  • Match the packaging format to your product size and courier dimension limits.
  • Finalize your print files in CMYK format before placing the main order.
  • Treat Black Friday and the holiday season as two separate packaging waves.

Plan your quantities

Start by pulling your exact sales data from the previous fourth quarter.

Look at the number of shipments you sent out and factor in your expected growth rate for this year. Once you have that baseline, add a safety buffer of around 15 to 20 percent to cover unexpected viral spikes or damaged stock.

Returns matter too. Customers send back items more frequently during the holidays, so you need enough boxes to handle reverse logistics and warehouse repackaging. Calculate your final number now so you know exactly what to order. Splitting your inventory into standard branded boxes and specific holiday designs helps you manage stock levels without overcommitting to seasonal prints that you cannot use in January.

Choose the product and format

Choose the right container for the specific items you sell.

If you ship fragile or premium goods, custom boxes like mailers or standard shipping cartons give you solid structural protection against rough handling. Size them as closely to your product as possible to avoid paying to ship empty air. Check your courier's dimensional weight limits so you do not cross into a higher pricing tier just because your box is half an inch too wide.

For clothing or non-fragile items, try mailing bags as a lighter, space-saving alternative. They cost less to ship and take up significantly less room on your warehouse shelves. If you stick to two or three standardized sizes across your entire product catalog, you make packing faster and easier for your fulfillment team.

Prepare the design

Have your artwork files ready before you place the order.

Ask your designer to set the file to the CMYK color space, as this is the standard for digital printing. At Packhelp, we use CMYK for full-color mailer and product boxes, so your brand colors translate correctly from screen to cardboard. Check that your PDF includes the correct bleed margins so no white edges appear after cutting.

Order a small test batch to check the print quality and fit. Since we produce custom packaging from 30 pieces, you can easily verify the colors and structural dimensions before committing to a large run. You can also add FSC and ISEGA certifications to your packaging designs, as we source materials and produce everything locally in Europe. Lock in these details early to avoid file rejections that slow down production.

Set your timing

Map out your order schedule now.

Treat Black Friday and the December holidays as two separate demand waves. Black Friday calls for high-volume, durable shipping materials to handle major sales spikes. The weeks leading up to late December are better served by festive designs aimed at gift buyers.

You need to order your Christmas packaging long before you actually need it in the warehouse. Supply chains always get crowded in Q4. While unprinted boxes take 7 days to produce, custom orders take longer during peak season. We deliver across 8 European markets within 3 to 5 business days, so factor that transit time into your plan. Lock in your delivery slots early so your warehouse team has materials ready when the first wave hits, and use a dedicated guide to pick a reliable packaging supplier for the Q4 season.

Secure your stock

Put a backup plan in place for the busiest weeks.

Even with perfect math, a sudden trend can wipe out your packaging inventory. Keep a separate stash of plain, unprinted boxes or standard mailers as your emergency stock. If you run out of custom packaging, you can still ship products on time. Follow a clear contingency playbook on what to do when you run out of packaging or your supplier is late so your operations team knows exactly how to react.

It also pays to stock up on smaller items like custom tape, tissue paper, or stickers. These extras take up very little warehouse space but let you add branding to a plain box if your main supply runs dry. They work well for upselling gift-wrapping services at checkout too. Having a secondary option ready keeps your fulfillment line moving no matter what happens with your primary inventory.

Summary

Running a smooth Q4 operation comes down to math, correct file preparation, and ordering ahead of the rush. Tick off these steps, finalize your numbers, and get your materials into production. Ready to check this off your list? Plan your Q4 packaging today.
