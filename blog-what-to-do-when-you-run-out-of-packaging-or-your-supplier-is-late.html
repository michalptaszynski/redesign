What to Do When You Run Out of Packaging or Your Supplier Is Late

Out of packaging or your supplier is late for Q4? Switch to standard sizes and simpler print, brand plain boxes with stickers and tape, and keep a buffer.

In short: Running out of boxes right before Black Friday calls for quick, practical moves. Switch to plain stock boxes and add simple branding like stickers or tape. At Packhelp, we produce plain unprinted boxes in just 7 days, or you can order digitally printed custom boxes starting from just 30 pieces to bridge the gap.

Q4 is the last moment you want to see empty shelves in your warehouse. Missing boxes mean delayed shipments, frustrated customers, and lost revenue during your most profitable weeks of the year. Let’s fix the emergency first with quick, workable steps, then build a solid strategy so your fulfillment line does not stall again.

What to do when you run out of packaging?

Your first move is to secure plain, standard-sized boxes from any available stock so your fulfillment line keeps moving today.

Avoid waiting weeks for a complex custom print run if orders are already piling up on your loading dock. Customers care far more about getting their holiday gifts on time than about a beautifully printed box that shows up three days late.

You can still keep your brand visible without full custom printing. Switch to blank cardboard and apply custom labels with your logo or a short thank-you note. Applying a sticker takes seconds on the packing line and instantly turns a generic carton into a recognizable package.

Another fast alternative is using branded packing tape on those neutral boxes. It gives your parcel a professional look right away while also keeping the contents secure.

Once you have emergency supplies in hand, organize your pending shipments strategically. Prioritize high-value orders or those with guaranteed express delivery dates. Use the last of your custom boxes for premium products, and ship standard items in the emergency branded plain boxes. If your catalog includes items that do not strictly need rigid corrugated cardboard, move them to other formats from our catalog, such as mailer bags or paper envelopes. That way you can reserve your rigid boxes for fragile or heavy goods.

What to do when your packaging supplier is late?

Start by contacting your vendor and clarifying exactly what part of the order they can deliver right now.

Often, a delay affects only a specific custom size or a complex finish, while standard items might already be sitting on their warehouse floor. Ask for a partial shipment of whatever is completed today and verify that this partner still fits your needs using this guide to choosing a packaging supplier for Q4.

If your custom design is creating the production bottleneck, simplify the request. Switching to standard shipping boxes can drastically cut production time compared to manufacturing custom tooling and cutting dies. At Packhelp, standard plain boxes have a production time of 7 days, and we deliver within the European Union in 3 to 5 business days.

You can also reduce print complexity to speed things up. Full CMYK digital printing on mailer boxes and product boxes is fast because it does not require physical printing plates. We offer this digital print option starting from just 30 pieces. Ordering a small emergency batch of 30 or 50 digitally printed boxes helps you cover this week’s orders while you wait for your main offset or flexographic bulk order to arrive. For our Eco and Eco White boxes, we offer fast single-color digital printing, while Eco Color boxes use UV printing.

Ask your supplier about express shipping or a split delivery. Have them send a pallet of ready boxes via priority courier to keep your warehouse running, and let the rest of the bulk order follow via standard freight once it is completed.

How to prevent packaging shortages for Black Friday and Christmas?

To prevent shortages, calculate your Q4 packaging needs based on historical sales data and then add a mandatory safety buffer of at least 15 to 20 percent.

Relying on guesswork leads straight to stockouts when the orders start spiking. Support this planning with a dedicated Q4 packaging checklist and turn it into a recurring process for every peak season.

Begin with last year’s numbers. Check the exact volume shipped between mid-November and late December. Treat Black Friday and Christmas as two separate peaks with different requirements. Black Friday usually needs standard, durable transit packaging for high-volume electronics or apparel. Christmas shipments often require specialized holiday packaging options with festive designs and a more thoughtful unboxing experience.

Focus your forecasting effort on your bestsellers. If three specific box sizes cover 80 percent of your total shipments, overstock those particular dimensions. Having too many standard boxes is a manageable inconvenience; running out of your most used size can freeze your entire operation.

Place your peak season orders months in advance. Our production takes place entirely in Europe, and we supply packaging made from FSC and ISEGA certified materials. Getting your order into the production queue early helps ensure you have physical stock in your warehouse long before carrier networks get congested in November.

How to prepare your supply chain for the peak season?

Prepare your supply chain by mapping out a clear timeline with your primary supplier early in Q3 and keeping an active relationship with a backup vendor.

Relying on a single source for all your packaging materials is a major risk during the holiday rush. If one factory faces a delay, you want a second supplier ready to print your designs quickly.

Approve your print proofs and designs as early as you can. At Packhelp, you can use our online editor with a 3D preview to finalize your design without endless email chains and revisions. Approving the design in September means your boxes can be printed and shipped before factory floors hit maximum capacity.

Track your inventory levels weekly, not monthly. Set strict reorder points in your warehouse management system. When a specific box size drops below a three-week supply, trigger an automatic reorder. Do not wait until the pallets look empty or the picker flags a shortage.

It also helps to split large volume orders into manageable batches. Ask your supplier to deliver the first 30 percent of your packaging in October and the remaining 70 percent in early November. This keeps your warehouse from overflowing while still guaranteeing enough stock to start the Black Friday rush. Stay in close contact with your account manager to track these deliveries across the 8 European markets we ship to. Consolidating your supplies—ordering boxes, mailers, paper tubes, tissue paper fillers, and stickers from one provider—makes tracking inbound freight far easier to manage.
